What if the delay line was the neural net?

The first electronic computers stored bits as sound in tubes of mercury. We use the same trick to compute: a tapped delay line whose taps are weighted -1, 0 or +1 recognises radio modulations, and you can race it against four other classifiers below. [[DRAFT]]

Read this first

  • Nothing here is measured. Every number comes from simulation, from a bit-true fixed-point model checked against Verilog RTL, or from a hardware design that has not been fabricated.
  • The board is designed, not built. No claim is made that it works.
  • Accuracy is on our own synthetic 8-class set (128 samples, 8 per symbol). It is not comparable with RadioML results.
  • We are not first. Tapped-line recurrent nets for RF (Pour 2025; Merton 2026), ternary modulation recognition (Woo 2024) and tapped delay-line learning (Tank 1987) came before; see the lineage.
  • Energy figures are labelled estimates built from cited per-operation costs, not measurements of this design.

Sound in a tube of mercury

[[DRAFT]] Mercury delay lines held the memory of the EDVAC generation: a pulse goes in at one end of a tube, comes out later, and is re-injected. Tapping the line along its length gives you something else: a weighted sum of the signal at several delays, which is a filter, and with learned weights, a layer of a neural network.

[[DRAFT]] Our version keeps the weights to a polarity. A tap is wired in as +1, wired in inverted as -1, or left off. A tanh soft limiter stands in for the neuron, an RC stage pools, and a small digital readout picks one of eight modulations. On our synthetic set the ideal model scores and the bit-true fixed-point version .

The maths, lightly

[[DRAFT]] Layer equation: u[o,n] = Σi,k W[o,i,k] x[i, n-k·d] and y[o,n] = tanh((u[o,n] + b[o]) / Vsat). Delay is length over phase velocity; on the board it is an LC ladder. What passive parts cannot do (gain, nonlinearity, softmax) is why the design is a minimal hybrid.
