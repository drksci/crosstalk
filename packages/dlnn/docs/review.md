# Independent review of the DLNN documentation and figures

Reviewer: independent, read-only (this file is the only one written). Date: 2026-11-03. Sources: `out/final-w32/`, `out/final-w8/`, `out/narma10-m4a/`,
`models/`, `dlnn/` code, `tests/`, `docs/figures/`. Small checks were re-run (listed in section c); the 11-minute pipeline was not.

## (a) Verdict

The numbers are honest and reproducible as documented. Every headline number I checked in `README.md`, `docs/README.md` and
`docs/results.md` matches `compare.json`, the tier JSONs, `E_rtl.json`, `r_ideal30.txt`, `eval_report.json` and the train reports to the
printed precision. The tier labelling is unusually careful. The C tier JSON says in capitals that its 4000-example numbers "are NOT SPICE
results" and that layers 2-3 are extrapolated. E says the RTL ran on a subset only. The LLM is never credited with analysis, and its
flawed final commentary is shown unedited with the flaws listed.

The main weakness is the story told about why C and R fall short. The docs, the figure captions and fig. 5 put C's 20-point gap down to
"32 dB/us with no repeaters". The data say otherwise: most of the gap (14 of 20 points) comes from the C tier's default per-layer gain
trim. With no compensation at all, C scores 0.804; with ideal per-tap repeaters it scores 0.805, no better. results.md and honesty.md do
disclose the mode dependence. But README.md calls it "part" of the gap, tiers.md says the opposite of the data, and the docs say the cause
"was not investigated", although it is easy to show (below). I would show this to a sceptical engineer once the attribution text is fixed.
As it stands, a careful reader will find the C headline number and its explanation in conflict with the project's own diagnostics file.
